## The stackup

PCBWay's own 4-layer 1.61 mm construction at 1 oz outer copper, which is this board's class. It had never been chosen: the board carried no `(stackup ...)` block at all, so KiCad's defaults were in force and the dielectric height — the number every impedance and coupling figure depends on — was whatever KiCad assumed. `verify.check_stackup()` is what holds it now.

## Does a routing cell fit between two SOIC pins?

The question the routing pass was left with, and the reason the answer turned out not to be a finer grid. A SOIC pad is 0.6 mm across on a 1.27 mm pitch, so there is 0.67 mm of copper-free space between two pins. A cell reliably falls in it only when the legal window is at least one grid pitch wide, and the finest legal pitch is `track + clearance` — so the test reduces to `gap >= 3 x clearance + 2 x track`, which does not mention the pitch at all.

| class | track / clearance | copper | window | pitch floor | a cell fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fitted | 0.20 / 0.20 | either weight | +0.070 mm | 0.40 mm | **no**, at any pitch |
| 2 oz minimum | 0.15 / 0.15 | 2 oz outer | +0.220 mm | 0.30 mm | **no**, at any pitch |
| 1 oz minimum | 0.09 / 0.09 | 1 oz outer only | +0.400 mm | 0.18 mm | yes |

**So the corridor opens only at JLCPCB's finest multilayer class, which is available at 1 oz outer copper and not at 2 oz.** That is not a routing decision, it is a decision to give up 2 oz as an option -- and both are history. route.py is deleted; KiCadRoutingTools closed this board at the fitted class without a finer grid, by ripping up and re-routing the nets in the way, so it is ordered at that class.
